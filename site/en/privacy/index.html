At a glance

StagePane does not collect personal data.

StagePane has no account, advertising, analytics, telemetry, third-party runtime SDK, or publisher-operated server. In the Mac App Store build, Apple StoreKit handles only StagePane Pro product and purchase information. Every build uses App Sandbox and has no network client entitlement.

  • No recordingChosen screen frames are processed in memory only for display.
  • No automatic savingOne Stage PNG is exported only when you explicitly choose Copy or Save Audience Image.
  • No uploadStagePane does not send your screen or usage to the publisher or any third party.

Screen content

StagePane accesses screen content only after you open Apple's system content-sharing picker and explicitly choose one window, application, or display. Choosing an application can include multiple windows owned by that application; choose one window when you want the narrowest scope. Free supports two independently pausable and removable sources; the one-time StagePane Pro purchase supports up to four.

Chosen frames are displayed locally in parallel in “StagePane Stage,” the clean window intended for sharing, and on the Canvas in “Stage Workspace.” Sources, Stage Settings, Appearance, Permissions, Privacy, and About are available from the Workspace sidebar.

Crop changes only the region included in the local Stage composition and an explicit Audience PNG. It does not narrow Apple's picker approval or the ScreenCaptureKit stream: whenever that stream runs, ScreenCaptureKit handles the complete selected window, application, or display. Pause stops the stream; Remove or Stop All ends its capture session. The complete source appears in the private Workspace while its crop is edited. Crop rectangles remain only in app memory, including while a disconnected layer waits for Select Again, until you Remove that layer, choose Stop All, or quit StagePane.

Each choice grants access only to that selected content for that capture session. StagePane does not request separate, broad Screen Recording access. Canceling the picker adds nothing. Removing a source or choosing Stop All ends the corresponding streams and discards their displayed frames.

StagePane does not:

  • record or encode selected frames;
  • write them to disk, logs, the clipboard, or a database, except for the explicit one-shot PNG export described below;
  • perform OCR, object recognition, or AI processing;
  • transmit them to StagePane, its publisher, or any third party; or
  • capture system audio or microphone audio.

Laser pointer

When you choose the Laser pointer style, StagePane reads only the current pointer position needed to place one dot over the frontmost Stage source. If that source is paused, no dot appears and StagePane does not fall back to a source behind it. Draw hides the audience pointer and stops pointer-location sampling until Arrange or Crop resumes. StagePane does not retain pointer history or write coordinates to disk; each sampled position is continuously discarded and cleared when capture stops, the source changes, or Draw begins. Laser mode itself does not install an event tap or request Accessibility or Input Monitoring permission.

Workspace modes

Arrange

Compose the Stage freely

Drag, resize, and reorder two sources in Free or up to four with StagePane Pro. The same composition appears in Workspace and on the public Stage.

Crop

Choose what appears on Stage

Inspect the complete source in the private Workspace, then show only the framed region on the public Stage and in Audience PNGs. The capture scope itself does not narrow.

Draw

Annotate the public Stage

Use a pen, highlighter, and partial eraser to draw audience-visible annotations from Workspace. Draw hides the audience pointer, and ink stays in memory.

StagePane does not forward clicks, keys, or drags to source apps, and it requests no Accessibility or Input Monitoring permission.

Crop, Draw, Pause, and Curtain behavior

Draw keeps a bounded set of normalized vector strokes in memory and renders the same ink in the private Workspace and public Stage. It does not automatically write, upload, or analyze ink. An explicit Audience Stage screenshot includes visible ink as rasterized pixels. Stop All or removal of the final source clears the in-memory drawing.

During normal playback, frames are discarded as display advances. Pausing one source stops its stream and makes that layer transparent in the Stage, private Workspace, and Audience PNG output while retaining only its placement, crop, and z-order. Resume keeps it transparent until a new complete frame arrives. Remove deletes that source's pixels and retained layer state; Stop All does this for every source.

Privacy Curtain is not Stop Capture.

The Curtain visually covers only the public Stage. It does not pause active sources, and the private Workspace remains visible so you can prepare the composition.

The “Keep Private” label on Stage Workspace is workflow guidance, not a technical privacy boundary. Sharing the StagePane application or a full display can expose that window. When it must stay hidden, choose the exact “StagePane Stage” window in your meeting app.

User-initiated screenshots

StagePane does not take screenshots on a timer, in the background, at launch, or in response to capture changes. It creates one lossless PNG of the clean Audience Stage only when you choose Copy Audience Image or Save Audience Image… in Stage Workspace.

The PNG uses the selected Stage pixel dimensions and includes what the audience Stage currently shows: shared content or Curtain, ink, mark, the safe-area guide when enabled, and the pointer when visible. It excludes Workspace navigation and controls, title-bar chrome, and unrelated application windows.

The image is composed locally from the latest pixels already approved through Apple's content-sharing picker and StagePane's own artwork. This path does not enumerate unrelated windows, request new screen permission, record video, or use the network.

Copy places the PNG on the macOS general pasteboard. Other local applications and clipboard managers may access it according to their own behavior. Save opens the macOS save panel and writes only to the location you select; canceling writes nothing. After the copy or write finishes, StagePane keeps no separate screenshot history or screenshot file of its own.

Settings stored on your device

StagePane stores interface preferences such as aspect preset, theme, pointer style, color, size and glow, drawing tool/color/ink width/eraser size, mark and safe-area visibility, Curtain message, and window behavior in local UserDefaults on your Mac.

It does not persist your chosen source, window title, application name, screenshot history, chosen screenshot path, or meeting information. Standard window-frame preferences may include the positions and sizes of the Stage and Workspace windows, but not their pixels. A PNG created through Save is your chosen local file, not an app preference or hidden retained copy.

Source titles may appear transiently in the in-memory source list while capture is active. Dragged positions, sizes, and crop rectangles remain only in app memory for the current StagePane run. A disconnected layer retains them for Select Again; Remove, confirmed Stop All, or quitting StagePane discards them. You can remove preferences by deleting the app's local container or using available macOS app-data controls.

Permissions

The persistent Permissions view explains that Apple's system picker grants screen-sharing access per selection and per capture session. The normal Add Source flow does not present a global Screen Recording status, request separate broad Screen Recording access, or require a System Settings step. Removing a source or choosing Stop All ends its session-scoped access. StagePane remains usable as a holding share window before you select a source.

Network and third parties

StagePane does not send screen content, screenshots you copy or save, or usage information to the publisher or any third party. In the Mac App Store build, it uses Apple StoreKit to load StagePane Pro product information, purchase or restore it, and check purchase status. No screen content or usage information is sent through this process.

The meeting app that shares StagePane Stage is separate software. Its transmission, recording, account, and data practices are governed by that provider's terms and privacy policy. macOS, the App Store, and Apple frameworks are governed by Apple's terms and privacy information.

Retention, disclosure, sale, and tracking

Because StagePane does not collect screen content or personal data, it has no server-side retention, disclosure, sale, cross-context behavioral advertising, or tracking process. A screenshot you explicitly copy or save exists only in the local pasteboard or chosen file location and is not retained by the publisher.

The app privacy manifest declares that tracking is false and collected data types are empty. StagePane is a general productivity utility and is not directed to children.

Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated if StagePane's features or data practices change. The updated policy will be published with a revised effective date. Material changes will be communicated through an appropriate product or project channel.
